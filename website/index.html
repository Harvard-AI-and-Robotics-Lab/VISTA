Abstract

Blindness and low vision limit many people’s ability to complete daily tasks, motivating AI assistants that provide useful guidance. Yet, existing benchmarks do not adequately test whether vision models can provide effective everyday assistance. In particular, i) existing benchmarks primarily rely on visual and textual inputs, with limited support for other modalities that are increasingly available in egocentric wearable devices such as gaze, inertial measurements, spatial trajectories, and others; and ii) existing evaluations emphasize scene understanding and question answering, with limited coverage of goal-conditioned guidance for blind and low-vision users across everyday tasks. To bridge this gap, we introduce VISTA (Visually Impaired Scene and Task Annotations), a multimodal egocentric benchmark for goal-oriented assistance for blind and low-vision users. VISTA contains ∼1,000 egocentric recordings captured with Project Aria glasses; each recording provides synchronized sensor streams, six of which serve as model inputs, and three different types of annotation, covering ten task categories (TC), from object localization to hazard detection. We evaluate 14 representative vision-language models zero-shot on VISTA and design a lightweight multimodal model baseline that integrates all six modalities. Experimental results show that, although existing models can often describe egocentric scenes, they remain limited in generating goal-directed assistive guidance. VISTA thus provides a benchmark for evaluating multimodal models that could improve everyday accessibility.

Sensor modalities of the Aria glasses. VISTA records synchronized RGB video, stereo SLAM and eye-tracking cameras, audio, IMU, barometer, and magnetometer.

Explore the VISTA Dataset

VISTA contains 997 egocentric clips across ten task categories, split into 497 training, 50 validation, and 450 test clips. Each clip includes scene descriptions, question–answer pairs, and goal–instruction pairs to support scene understanding, answering questions, and goal-oriented assistance. Reported results use 8 uniformly sampled RGB frames plus task text, evaluated on 1,828 items from 450 test clips: 450 scene descriptions, 510 Q&A items, and 868 guidance items. Judge: GPT-6 Luna (gpt-6-luna), with high reasoning effort and the rubric below.

Multimodal baseline

We test whether synchronized sensor streams improve answer quality by training language LoRA adapters and a sensor-fusion network on VISTA in two stages, using 8 then 28 sensor tokens. Answers are generated with greedy decoding and evaluated with the same judge and rubric as the zero-shot baselines.
